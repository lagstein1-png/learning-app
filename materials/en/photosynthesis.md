---
title: How Plants Make Food
language: en
level: grade-5
---
Photosynthesis is the biochemical process by which green plants, algae and certain bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy that is stored in glucose. The process takes place predominantly in the chloroplasts, which are specialised organelles containing the pigment chlorophyll, the substance responsible for the characteristic green colouration of foliage. During photosynthesis, carbon dioxide absorbed through microscopic pores called stomata is combined with water drawn up from the roots, and, utilising the energy captured from sunlight, these raw materials are transformed into glucose and oxygen. The oxygen is subsequently released into the atmosphere as a by-product, which is precisely why forests are frequently described as the lungs of the planet. It is estimated that photosynthesis produces approximately 100 billion tonnes of organic matter every year, sustaining virtually every food chain on Earth.
